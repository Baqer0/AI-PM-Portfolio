01 · The problem

Patients live between two worlds.

Clinical care happens in short appointments. Everyday life happens everywhere else — during travel, at work, late at night, and between flare-ups.

MyCCAngel was designed to close that gap with a trusted patient community, centralized resources, practical support, and personalized AI-driven experiences.

02 · The product

Designed around the patient journey.

The product combined verification, onboarding, personalization, community, resources and everyday-life assistance rather than treating AI as a standalone feature.

03 · AI / ML

AI was built into the product loop.

The strongest AI decisions were not about adding a chatbot. They were about using trustworthy signals to solve verification, cold-start personalization, and useful guidance.

Medical report verification

Users uploaded medical reports that moved through OCR, text cleaning and an SVM classifier before the verification decision. The model was trained on a manually labeled dataset of 200 reports and achieved approximately 91% accuracy on a held-out test split.

Recommendation engine

The cold-start problem was solved at the product level: the onboarding questionnaire became the initial personalization signal. Diagnosis, status, severity, daily-life impact and support needs were mapped into recommendation logic before behavioral data existed.

AI healthcare assistant

I defined the requirements and designed the full assistant experience. The development team built a functional conversational NLP/ML pipeline. The next step was IBD-specific training on a curated, trusted knowledge base, with guardrails to prevent diagnosis or treatment recommendations.

04 · Product decisions

Every feature had to answer a hard question.

01

Trust before growth

Medical verification was a core trust mechanic. A smaller verified community was more valuable than maximizing registrations.

02

Solve cold start in onboarding

New users needed value immediately, so the questionnaire was designed to generate useful personalization before behavioral data existed.

03

Prioritize everyday problems

Practical needs such as toilet access mattered as much as educational content because they directly affect daily quality of life.

04

Don't build partnerships prematurely

Directories and partner ecosystems were postponed until a working product could demonstrate value to healthcare organizations.

05

Don't ship unsafe AI

The assistant was explicitly scoped away from diagnosis and treatment, with trusted-content retrieval and escalation planned before launch.

06

Prioritization over feature volume

The MVP focused resources on the highest-risk, highest-value flow: signup → verification → onboarding → recommendations.

05 · Honest reflection

Why development paused before launch.

MyCCAngel reached substantial implementation before development paused during team restructuring. I consider pausing the launch the responsible product decision: healthcare AI requires clinical validation, trusted content, appropriate partnerships and sustainable execution capacity.

Clinical validationHealthcare AI needs stronger validation and safety processes before public launch.
Trusted contentThe assistant needed a curated IBD knowledge base rather than unsupported open-ended generation.
Team capacityContributor attrition reduced the team's ability to complete the remaining backend and launch work.
06 · What I learned

Five lessons from building an AI product in healthcare.

Trust is the feature.In healthcare AI, credibility is part of the product.
Cold start is a product problem.Good onboarding can create useful signals before models have behavior data.
Preprocessing matters.A clean data pipeline can make lightweight ML practical.
Prioritization beats production.Deciding what not to build was some of the highest-leverage work.
AI starts with product thinking.The best AI feature is the one connected to a real user problem.
